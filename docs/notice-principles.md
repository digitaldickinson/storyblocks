# Build → Notice → Rebuild

The central learning interaction of StoryBlocks.

**BUILD**: make editorial decisions by physically constructing a story.
**NOTICE**: step back and look at relationships and consequences in what you've built.
**REBUILD**: respond by changing the construction, or deliberately leave it.

Then repeat.

StoryBlocks does not tell students what the correct construction is. Its job is to make significant features of *their* construction harder to overlook. A notice means *look at what your construction is doing*. It does not mean *this is wrong and you should fix it*. The student keeps editorial agency.

## Responsibility

| Who | Does |
|---|---|
| Student | Makes editorial decisions |
| StoryBlocks | Makes relationships and consequences visible |
| Tutor and peers | Help interrogate the significance of those decisions |

StoryBlocks should prompt conversation, not replace it.

## 1. Build: keep it playful

The normal state is a construction toy. Students add, move, resize and remove bricks, leave gaps, solo layers and turn the model, with no warnings appearing. Nothing is analysed while they build. They need to get their own editorial thinking out first, not build to please the software.

## 2. Notice: a deliberate action

A **Notice** button. (Not Check, Analyse, Validate, Review, Improve or Score.) Pressing it means *help me look again at what I've built*. It never opens by itself.

*Status: built.*

## 3. Observations and questions, not verdicts

Good:

> **Writing to pictures.** The script carries on for 4 studs after the last picture. What would the audience see while it's read?

> **The place itself.** Nothing in the sound layer is natural sound, actuality or atmos. What would the viewer hear if they were standing there?

> **Claim.** Nothing labelled challenge comes after this claim in the structure layer. Does it need challenging?

Avoid:

> ⚠ Your script is too long. · ❌ Natural sound missing. · You should add a challenge. · Your package could be improved by…

"Yes, that's deliberate" is a successful response.

### Structural versus interpretive

StoryBlocks can state a structural relationship with confidence ("the script extends 4 studs beyond the pictures"). It should be tentative about interpretation ("does it need challenging?"), and it should never instruct ("this claim must be challenged"). The more interpretive the issue, the more the notice is a question.

**Messages state only what the build shows**: lengths in studs, which bricks, "at the start" or "at the end". There are no stud numbers, because students never see numbers on the board.

*Status: built. Each rule is a "Look" (structural) or a "Question" (interpretive).*

## 4. Don't overwhelm

Show about three notices, with the rest behind "Show more". Order: Look before Question, each group left to right along the build. (The software doesn't rank "usefulness", which would itself be a judgement.)

*Status: built.*

## 5. Connect notices to the build

Hovering a notice highlights the bricks it's about and outlines the stretch of studs; clicking keeps the highlight. No warning colours. The aim is *notice → look at the construction*, not *notice → read a feedback panel*. The model stays the main interface.

*Status: built.*

## 6. Rebuild: straight back to the bricks

No acknowledging, marking resolved, writing a reflection, accepting a recommendation or "apply fix". No automatic repair. A student might shorten the script, lengthen a picture, add a shot, restructure, or leave it unchanged; all are valid.

*Status: built.*

## 7. Repeatable, not live

After a change, the open notices go stale: they dim, and the panel offers "Notice again". They never update live, which would feel like validation. The loop is iterative experiment, not optimising against rules.

*Status: built.*

## 8. Examples are things to investigate

The examples are deliberately imperfect. They're framed as "Someone built this. Explore it: what do you notice? Then press Notice." Ideally students look first, then compare what they noticed with what StoryBlocks noticed. The software is not the definitive interpretation.

*Status: built.*

## 9. No scoring

No percentages, traffic lights, completeness scores, "good journalism" meters, pass/fail or model answers. A build with no notices is not therefore good journalism: nothing the system looks for is showing, that's all. A build with several notices is not therefore poor.

When there are none, the panel says: "Nothing stands out to StoryBlocks right now. That doesn't mean it's finished, only that none of the things it looks for are showing."

## Design test for any new notice or feature

1. **Does it help the student see something about their own construction they might otherwise have overlooked?** If yes, it probably belongs.
2. **Does it tell the student what editorial decision to make?** If yes, reconsider it.

## Deferred on purpose

Discussed, not built, pending how students actually use plain Notice:

- **"That's on purpose" flags**: a small flag planted on the build that silences a notice while it's there. No text, nothing to mark. (For silence, a SIL brick already does this.)
- **"Since last time"**: on Notice again, first say neutrally what changed ("your script now ends with the pictures"), giving deliberate choices and changes equal weight.

Considered and rejected:

- **A written reason, exported for a tutor.** It turns reflection into a marking artefact.
